## Profile

I am a PhD candidate and Research Associate at the News and Media Research Centre, University of Canberra. My research examines how multicultural and multilingual communities navigate complex news environments and how news use and trust shape belonging, settlement, civic participation and social cohesion.

My doctoral research focuses on trust across the multiple news systems used by multicultural Australians. My wider research encompasses journalism, communication infrastructure, multicultural communication, media trust and multicultural audiences.

Before entering academia, I worked as a broadcast journalist at Beijing Television. My combined experience in journalism, multilingual research, university teaching and professional translation informs an interdisciplinary research program focused on news, trust and communication in culturally diverse societies.

## PhD Research

### Trust in News and Multicultural Social Cohesion

My doctoral research examines how multicultural Australians navigate and evaluate English-language Australian news, community-language news and news from their countries or regions of origin. It investigates how trust develops across these overlapping news systems and how news use connects with settlement, belonging, political efficacy and civic participation. By moving beyond the assumption that audiences inhabit a single national media system, the project develops a more culturally responsive account of news trust and communication infrastructure in multicultural Australia.

The project draws on:

- An original survey of 231 participants from multicultural communities in Australia.
- Nineteen in-depth interviews with participants from diverse cultural and migration backgrounds.
- Communication infrastructure theory, uses and gratifications, and acculturation theory.
- Integrated quantitative and qualitative analysis.

### The Rise of Mistrust: Digital platforms and trust in news media
[Project website](https://www.canberra.edu.au/uc-research/faculty-research-centres/nmrc/major-projects/the-rise-of-mistrust)

Funded by the Australian Research Council through Discovery Project DP210100157 (2021–2024), this multi-method and cross-national project examined how digital platforms reshape the mechanisms through which trust and mistrust influence audience behaviour. Its program combined longitudinal analysis, a cross-country experiment and qualitative research to generate evidence relevant to journalism practice and media policy. As a doctoral scholarship recipient and research team member embedded in the project, I contributed to its evidence review, research design, data analysis, project coordination and collaborative publications.

- Conducted a **systematic literature review** across four databases, screening a corpus of more than 1,000 records.
- Supported research project management, including data management, meeting coordination and research administration.
- Contributed to experimental research design and the development of interview protocols.
- Contributed to longitudinal research design using Digital News Report data.
- Conducted qualitative data analysis using **ATLAS.ti**.
- Conducted quantitative data analysis using **SPSS**.
- Contributed to collaborative article development on news trust, mistrust and audience disengagement.
